For months, investigators working on the Andromeda case had the same problem: there was no record of the yacht’s journey around the Baltic. So we rebuilt its route ourselves.

We analysed AIS data on MarineTraffic for hundreds of other yachts that were in Baltic ports in Germany, Denmark, Sweden and Poland between 6 and 26 September. We contacted their owners or captains, talked to port workers and other witnesses, and checked satellite images. That trails led us to Rustem A., a Kyiv businessman whose company had rented the boat.

When we called him, he compared journalists to prostitutes and demanded $5,000 for an hour of his time.

So my colleague and I went to Kyiv to ask him our questions in person. His neighbours, giving us directions, described his house as the most lavish, conspicuous and eye-catching one in the area.

It was in a gated community you couldn’t enter without a key. When we rang the bell, he came out into his front yard, started shouting, and made it very clear that we should leave. So we left.

We were halfway to the exit when I heard a car hurtling down the gravel road straight towards us. We jumped aside onto the lawn, and his dark BMW X3 stopped within half a metre of us.

He got out, enraged. He threatened us and wouldn’t let us leave. Then he snatched my phone out of my hands and smashed it against the ground, and did the same to my colleague’s phone.

We rushed to the exit, but it turned out we needed a key to get through the giant metal fence as well. So we climbed over it. That’s how we got away, and how we cut our hands.
