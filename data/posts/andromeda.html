For months, investigators in every country working on the Andromeda case had the same problem: there was no record of the yacht’s journey around the Baltic. Most large yachts use AIS, a system that records a boat’s track on the water, including the ports it visits. Andromeda didn’t have one.

So we rebuilt its route ourselves. If Andromeda left no track, the boats around it did. We analysed AIS data on MarineTraffic for hundreds of other yachts that were in Baltic ports in Germany, Denmark, Sweden and Poland between 6 and 26 September. We contacted their owners or captains, talked to port workers and other witnesses, and checked satellite images. That trail led us to Rustem A., a Kyiv businessman whose company had rented the boat.

We knew German investigators had known his name for several months. We didn’t know whether they had ever contacted him. When we called him, he compared journalists to prostitutes and demanded $5,000 for an hour of his time. “My time is precious,” he sneered. The call didn’t last long.

So my colleague and I went to Kyiv to ask him our questions in person. His house was in a gated community you couldn’t enter without a key. We waited until a car was driving in and simply walked through while the gate was still open. His neighbours, giving us directions, described his house as the most lavish, conspicuous and eye-catching one in the area.

When we rang the bell, he came out into his front yard. He started shouting, made it very clear that we should leave, and disappeared back into the house. So we left.

We were halfway to the exit when I heard a car hurtling down the gravel road straight towards us. We jumped aside onto the lawn, and his dark BMW X3 stopped within half a metre of us.

He got out, enraged. He grabbed us, threatened us and wouldn’t let us leave. He wanted to know how we had found his address. Then he snatched my phone out of my hands and smashed it against the ground, and did the same to my colleague’s phone.

We rushed to the exit, but it turned out we needed a key to get through the giant metal fence as well. So we climbed over it. That’s how we got away, and how we cut our hands.
