Witness interviews are long: one transcript can run to hundreds of turns. I built a tool that reads them and flags the passages that may point to war crimes or crimes against humanity, such as the militarisation of schools or teaching only in Russian. Each suggestion comes with the exact quote, and a researcher keeps it, dismisses it or adds a note. The reviewed findings can be exported as a CSV.

Low-level design

  • Anonymisation: first, real names are swapped for tags like PERSON_1.
  • Speaker labelling: a batch of timestamped sections, plus the last two turns already labelled (for context), is sent to the model. The returned turns are aligned back to the original text with word alignment.
  • Classification: the labelled turns are cut into chunks of about 200 words, each ending before an interviewer turn, and each chunk is classified. Every call gets a taxonomy injected, in which each possible crime is described and proofread by a lawyer. The model returns findings structured by a JSON schema, which makes the prompt shorter and the answer structured. Each finding includes the issue, the exact quote, and what the witness actually says about it: supported, uncertain, hearsay or denied.
